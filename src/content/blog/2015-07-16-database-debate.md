As part of the Berlin <a href="http://toa.berlin/">Tech Open Air</a> events I want to the "<a href="http://www.eventbrite.com/e/database-debate-dcmn-tickets-17411536374">Database debate</a>" in DC Media networks. The two sides of the debate were Simon Willnauer, lead engineer of ElasticSearch and co-founder of <a href="https://www.elastic.co/">Elastic</a>and Carter Page, technical lead for Google BigTable.
<div></div>
The talk was hosted by DC Media networks employee and was navigated really well with nice questions and interactions between Willnauer and Page. However it was not a "Database debate" at least for two reasons. ElasticSearch currently does not brand itself as a database and as admitted by Willnauer is not mature enough. Therefore it was not a debate - no pros and cons, no one against the other. But rather two solutions for different problems which both somehow relate to the buzzword "big data".
<div>Some expected questions were asked - use-cases, road map and future features, bug fixes, comparison to other solutions. But also less expected questions - what would you do different if rebuilding the product, pitfalls of beginners and some more technical deep dive questions asked by the audience.</div>
<div>
<div>

One of the nice questions asked by the host was - "What is the weirdest usage you have seen to your product". Willnauer answered - "playing chess with it using near neighbor to compute the next step". Following <a href="https://aphyr.com/tags/jepsen">Call me maybe</a> project I though of "Call me checkmate" project - playing chess using different databases.

Overall, very nice and chill atmosphere. Although I'm not sure why it is called open air..
