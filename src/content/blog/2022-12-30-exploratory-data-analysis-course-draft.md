<p>Last week I gave an extended version of my talk about <a href="https://tomron.net/2022/09/15/think-outside-of-the-box-plot/">box plots</a> in <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/cohenoa/">Noa Cohen</a>'s Introduction to Data Science class at Azrieli College of Engineering Jerusalem. Slides can be found <a href="https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1dej92vTNRGAmMsgtOQIv73zNEjfzfifYSmwvYmU5k8I/edit?usp=sharing">here</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The students are 3rd and 4th-year students, and some will become data scientists and analysts. Their questions and comments and my experience with junior data analysts made me understand that a big gap they have in purchasing those positions and performing well is doing EDA - exploratory data analysis. This reminded me of the <a href="https://missing.csail.mit.edu/">missing semester of your CS education</a> - skills that are needed and sometimes perceived as common knowledge in the industry but are not taught or talked about in academia. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>"Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is the crucial process of using summary statistics and graphical representations to perform preliminary investigations on data in order to uncover patterns, detect anomalies, test hypotheses, and verify assumptions." (see more <a href="https://blog.eduonix.com/bigdata-and-hadoop/importance-exploratory-data-analysis-ml-modelling/">here</a>). EDA plays an important role in everyday life of anyone working with data - data scientists, analysts, and data engineers. It is often also relevant for managers and developers to solve the issues they face better and more efficiently and to communicate their work and findings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p></p>
