<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A sense of humor is one of the most underestimated leadership skills</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Following the recommendation in “The Great CEO Within” I listened to “<a href="https://www.amazon.com/The-One-Minute-Manager/dp/0688014291">The One Minute Manager</a>”. In one of the chapters, they mention the usage of Humor as a leadership tool. It is not about becoming a comedian but rather showing up as humans. Humor is a powerful and often overlooked tool.<br>Why it matters:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Humor helps build trust and rapport - people are more likely to engage and collaborate when they feel comfortable and connected.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It can reduce stress and tension, boosting well-being and performance.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Humor makes leaders more approachable and memorable, signaling confidence and emotional intelligence.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Shared laughter fosters psychological safety, helping teams voice ideas and take risks.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Of course, balance is key - humor should complement clarity and respect, not replace them. Too many jokes or poorly timed humor can actually backfire, so think of it as a strategic leadership tool, not a default setting. It’s about knowing when a light moment can lower defenses, reset the room, or simply remind everyone that work is done by humans, not robots.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
