<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With the growing adoption of AI and recent moves like <strong><a href="https://claude.com/blog/cowork-plugins">Anthropic’s Cowork plugin marketplace</a></strong>, there’s a popular narrative that <strong>traditional SaaS is dead</strong>. The implication is that the combination of AI agents + marketplaces will commoditize software entirely, and the old subscription paradigms won’t survive.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I tend to believe that’s an overstatement. SaaS isn’t dying - it’s <strong>evolving</strong>. What <em>is</em> under threat is SaaS as we’ve known it: long-term seat licenses, one-size-fits-all tiers, and feature-driven pricing. AI makes core functionality easier to replicate and access, pushing raw features toward commodity status. The value and pricing increasingly lie in <strong>outcomes, workflows, integrations, and customer trust</strong>. More than in the past, pricing is a strategic tool. See a similar conversion in David Ondrej's <a href="https://x.com/DavidOndrej1/status/2019126831761572169">post</a> on Twitter (link in the first comment)</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That’s why understanding the <em>possible pricing models</em> and their tradeoffs matters more than ever. I’ve been listening to Ulrik Lehrskov-Schmidt’s webinar series on <a href="https://youtu.be/aHkUYBPrOqs?si=IF-m_ziEsIlIB-3L"><strong>Agentic AI pricing </strong>-<strong>“Making Money in Uncertain Times.”</strong></a> In a world where both <strong>cost structures</strong> (compute, labor, infrastructure) and <strong>capabilities</strong> (model performance, automation) are shifting rapidly, we can’t anchor pricing to static assumptions. Instead, pricing needs to reflect <em>real value delivery</em>, signal predictable economics to customers, and align engineering decisions with business outcomes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This isn’t just a GTM or sales conversation - it’s also a core product and engineering conversation. How we design systems, how we think about metrics and outcomes, and how we package those outcomes for customers all influence pricing levers.</p>
