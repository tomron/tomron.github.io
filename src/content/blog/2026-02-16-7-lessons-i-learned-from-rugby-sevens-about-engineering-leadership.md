<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Fun fact about me - I played Rugby Sevens for years, including on a national team. It taught me more about engineering leadership than I expected. Small teams, huge space, high speed - Sevens and modern engineering run on the same dynamics.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Here are 7 lessons Sevens taught me about engineering leadership.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">1️⃣ It’s Not About Weight - It’s About Teamwork</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The first lesson I learned came from scrumming with a weight disadvantage but a huge coordination advantage. In Rugby (in 15s even more than in 7s), you don’t win scrums because your players are heavier. A team wins because: you bind together, you push at the same moment, you trust the rhythm.  In lineouts, timing matters more than height, and coordination matters more than individual strength.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In engineering, this shows up everywhere:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>A brilliant engineer misaligned with the team slows progress.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>A “lighter” team moving in sync outperforms a group of heavy hitters pulling in different directions.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Velocity is not the sum of individual forces. It’s a synchronized force.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson:</strong> Coordination multiplies talent.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">2️⃣ Communication should be Constant and Loud</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Rugby Sevens, silence is dangerous. Players shout constantly:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>“Inside!”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>“Switch!”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>“Up!”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>“Blind!”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It’s not noise - it’s alignment. There’s too much space and speed to assume others know what you’re thinking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an office, you get accidental alignment:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Body language</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Whiteboard moments</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Corridor clarifications</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In remote work, silence becomes ambiguity. If communication isn’t explicit and frequent, gaps appear.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><span style="margin: 0px;padding: 0px"><strong>Lesson:</strong>&nbsp;Constant communication creates alignment.</span> <span style="margin: 0px;padding: 0px">Alignment</span> brings results.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">3️⃣ You Can Only Pass Backwards</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In rugby, you’re only allowed to pass the ball backwards.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you want to move forward, you must either run forward yourself  or align with teammates who are already in motion</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can’t throw the ball ahead and hope someone figures it out. The constraint forces structure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>To gain ground, teams spread wide.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>They create overlap.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>They run support lines before they’re needed.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>They time short, precise passes.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You go forward by passing backward - and that only works with discipline. In engineering, this principle shows up everywhere. You can’t “pass forward” sloppily:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Product can’t throw half-defined specs over the wall</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Engineering can’t push messy code to QA</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Leadership can’t announce strategy without alignment</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When work is tossed ahead without positioning and support, the play breaks. Real velocity doesn’t come from heroic sprints. It comes from synchronized movement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Too many handoffs? You lose momentum.<br>Too few? You get isolated and tackled.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson:</strong> Sustainable forward progress requires structured alignment. Coordination creates speed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">4️⃣ Reset Between Games</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Sevens tournaments, you play multiple games on the same day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Win big? Reset.<br>Lose badly? Reset.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next kickoff has no memory.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Engineering teams often carry emotional baggage:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>A big launch → complacency</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>An outage → overreaction</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Strong teams don’t ignore outcomes - they process them quickly.<br>They celebrate briefly, learn fast, and show up focused for the next “game.”</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson:</strong> Don’t let yesterday’s result dictate today’s execution.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">5️⃣ Scoring Under the Posts vs Securing the Try</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In rugby, when a player breaks through, they often try to run closer to the center before grounding the ball.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Why? Because it makes the conversion kick easier.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But that extra effort increases the risk of being tackled and losing the try entirely.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In engineering:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Do we ship now?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Or optimize a bit more?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Refactor first?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Polish the UI further?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes we optimize the conversion and lose the try.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson:</strong> Secure value first. Optimize second.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">6️⃣ You Don’t Always Need More Resources - Just a Change of Angle</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Sevens, attacking the wide-open side is obvious. Great teams exploit the blind side instead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You don’t need more force.<br>You need better perspective.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In engineering:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Reframing a product problem</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Reorganizing teams instead of hiring more</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Solving a process issue instead of pushing harder</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes the breakthrough isn’t scaling effort - it’s shifting angle.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson:</strong> Strategy beats brute force.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">7️⃣ Commit Fast - Adjust Faster</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Rugby Sevens, hesitation kills. <br><br>Pause before a tackle, you miss. <br>Delay the pass, the overlap's gone. <br>Half-commit to a line break, and you get isolated.<br><br> The game rewards decisiveness.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Engineering leadership is the same. Over-analysis drains momentum. Once you pick a direction, the team needs full commitment - not tentative buy-in where everyone's still hedging. <br><br>But here's the nuance: when a defender overcommits, great players side-step. Decisiveness doesn't mean rigidity. You commit fully, and when new information shows up or the situation shifts, you adjust fast - without ego, without drama.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson</strong>: Commit hard. Adjust harder.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
